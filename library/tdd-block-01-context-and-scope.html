Block 1: Context and scope

Block 1 study guide: scope, naming caution, and the four open debates before any diagram.

Time: 45 minutes

Business Explanation

Business Explanation

Leaders hear "AI TRiSM" and assume there is a checklist they can audit. There is a useful checklist, and there is no certification against the phrase itself. This block teaches the boundary of the study day: which controls are in, which product tours are out, and which four arguments you must be able to state before you draw a box.

Goal

State what this architecture day will defend, what it will refuse to claim, and which debate each later view is allowed to settle.

Required reading

  • MISSION for the success tests.
  • CLAIMS-AND-DEBATES Debates 1 to 4 and the "cannot claim" list. Do not reread the whole evidence log.
  • source-ledger rows S1, S2, S8, S9 only, so you know which sentences are primary.
  • mindmap context branch.
  • Park Conceptual view until the retrieval check. The altitude rule on that note is the exit, not the warm-up.

What to learn

AI TRiSM is Gartner's name for a set of technical capabilities that support governance, trustworthiness, fairness, safety, reliability, security, privacy, and data protection [S1]. IBM's explainer quotes a glossary definition and is a secondary source [S8]. It is a market category. It is not ISO/IEC 42001 and it is not the NIST AI Risk Management Framework.

The intake said "four layers" and then listed explainability and model monitoring, ModelOps, application security, and privacy. That list is the pillar crosswalk vendors publish [S9] [S10]. A Gartner press release says the framework "comprises four layers" and does not name them [S1]. Secondary sources, citing a Market Guide this pack could not open, name those layers as AI governance, runtime inspection and enforcement, information governance, and infrastructure and stack [S9]. Say "secondary" every time you repeat those four names.

The day designs a vendor-neutral reference. No cloud, no SKU, no install. Vendors appear only in the integration inventory, and government-cloud support stays UNVERIFIED.

Out of scope: training a model, a product tutorial, certification cramming, and any claim that TRiSM itself has a conformance path. In scope: capabilities, a proposed component set, deployment zones, external systems, the AI data path, and failure names from OWASP and two talks.

Four debates travel with you. You do not need to win them in this block. You need to know the pack's working stance:

Debate Working stance for this day Do not upgrade it to
1 Pillars or layers Use layers to place controls. Keep pillars as a crosswalk. Both namings are secondary except the bare phrase "four layers" [S1] [S9]. "Gartner defines the layers as..."
2 What to design against Anchor conformance talk to ISO/IEC 42001 [S14] [S15]. Use NIST AI RMF for risk language [S2]. Use TRiSM as the capability map. NIST is voluntary [S2] [S3]. "We are certified to TRiSM."
3 Guardrails Runtime inspection is necessary and not sufficient [S7b] [S11] [S12]. A detection-rate claim. E3W is unrun.
4 Trustworthiness coverage NIST names seven characteristics [S2]. Safe, and fair with harmful bias managed, have no clearly named pillar. A finished map of NIST AI 600-1 risks.

Actions

  1. Write a two-sentence boundary in NOTES or in the margin of this note: what is inside the architecture, what is outside.
  2. Copy the four working stances into your own words. Attach one [S#] to each.
  3. Mark three sentences you will not say in a design review. Suggested bans: a primary list of Gartner layer names, a government-cloud claim, a measured guardrail catch rate.
  4. Skim the actor table in Conceptual view only after the retrieval check, and confirm you still have not written a product name.

Retrieval check

Closed book

In two sentences, what is inside this architecture day and what is outside it? Which structure will you draw with, and why is that not a Gartner quote?

Answer

Inside: capabilities and controls for trust, risk, and security of AI systems across catalog, data mapping, assurance, runtime enforcement, monitoring, lifecycle, privacy, and evidence. Outside: building models, single-vendor tutorials, and any certification against the TRiSM name. Draw with the four-layer placement because it names enforcement points, and label those names as secondary [S9] because [S1] says "four layers" without naming them. Keep the four pillars as the crosswalk [S9] [S10].

Exit criteria

  • Boundary statement written, with in-scope controls and out-of-scope product work.
  • Four debates named, each with a working stance and an [S#].
  • You can state the altitude rule you are about to follow in Conceptual view: capabilities and actors, no products.
  • You have not filled a SKU, a region, or a government-cloud cell.
