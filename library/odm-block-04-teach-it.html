Block 4: Teach it

Block 4: a five-minute teach-back and the reviewer questions that follow it.

Time: 30 minutes Goal: Deliver a five-minute teach-back and answer reviewer questions without the ledger open. Pack: AI-TRISM hub

Business Explanation

Business Explanation

The teach-back is the rehearsal for the design review. Five minutes is enough to separate market language from standards and law, and to show where controls sit. The questions afterward are the ones that expose a borrowed slide.

Sources

Speak from 01-core-concepts and 02-mental-models. Cite ids aloud: [S1], [S2], [S6], [S7b], [S14]. If you cannot remember an id, say "desk pass" and the document title. Do not invent a page.

Actions

  1. 5 minutes. Read the script outline once. Close it.
  2. 5 minutes. Speak the talk. Time it. Stay between 4:30 and 5:30.
  3. 15 minutes. Have someone ask, or self-ask, at least four questions from the list below. Answer in under 45 seconds each.
  4. 5 minutes. Write the sentence you stumbled on. That sentence is a Missed-it item.

No slides. One sheet of paper with four headings is allowed: Market, Voluntary, Certifiable, Statute. Nothing else.

Five-minute script outline

0:00 to 0:40. The decision. You are defending a reference architecture for one AI system. The page will show where controls sit and which document each control answers to. AI TRiSM is the placement map. It is Gartner's market category [S1].

0:40 to 1:40. Four authorities. Market: Gartner counts four layers of technical capabilities and lists outcomes including fairness, safety, security, and privacy. The press release does not name the layers. Voluntary: NIST AI RMF, functions Govern, Map, Measure, Manage, plus seven trustworthiness characteristics. AI 600-1 is the GenAI profile. Both are voluntary [S2] [S3]. Certifiable: ISO/IEC 42001 is the AIMS. Independent certification bodies certify. ISO does not [S14]. Statute: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, where it applies. General application 2 Aug 2026 [S6].

1:40 to 2:40. Where controls sit. Secondary sources name the layers governance, runtime inspection and enforcement, information governance, and infrastructure and stack [S9]. You use those names as a placement aid and you say they are secondary. Pillars, also secondary, are the crosswalk: explainability, ModelOps, application security, privacy [S10]. A control can serve a pillar and still need a layer.

2:40 to 3:40. What "enough security" means. Runtime inspection stays. It is not the whole design. OWASP lists seven mitigations for prompt injection. About two are filtering. The others are privilege, human approval, segregation of external content, and adversarial tests [S7b]. Red team before you trust the outer firewall [S11]. Each agent gets its own identity [S12]. Say the vendor interest on that talk in the same breath.

3:40 to 4:30. What the one-pager will contain. Tool categories, not SKUs: catalog, policy, gateway, agent identity broker, evaluation harness, evidence store. Evidence is what an ISO review and an auditor take away. The crosswalk ties a NIST subcategory to a 42001 control [S4].

4:30 to 5:00. What you will not claim. You will not claim Gartner published the layer names on the page you read. You will not claim a TRiSM certificate. You will not cite Ray 2026. You will not quote detection rates. Debate 4, fairness and safety against the pillar names, is still open.

Questions a reviewer will ask

Answer aloud. Then open the fold.

Gartner says the four layers are governance, runtime, information, and infrastructure. Correct?
Answer

Gartner's press release says four layers and does not name them [S1]. Those four names are what Zscaler and similar secondary pages attribute to the Market Guide [S9]. The Market Guide PDF is paywalled. Say the names as secondary.

Can we get certified to AI TRiSM?
Answer

This ledger has no certification path for TRiSM. Certification in the pack attaches to ISO/IEC 42001 through independent certification bodies. ISO does not certify organizations [S14] [S15]. NIST's framework is voluntary [S2].

The AI firewall product covers application security. Can we stop there?
Answer

Keep the inspection point. Add least privilege, human approval for high-risk actions, segregation of retrieved content, and a red team [S7b] [S11]. The CSA talk says filters cannot cover an unbounded input space and points at agent identity. The speaker's employer sells detection [S12].

Do the four pillars cover fairness and safety?
Answer

NIST lists safe, and fair with harmful bias managed, among seven characteristics [S2]. The secondary pillar names do not include those words. Gartner's press-release outcomes do include fairness and safety [S1]. The map from outcomes to pillars is unfinished. Debate 4 stays open.

Quote the Ray 2026 review for the room.
Answer

You cannot. The paper is unread. Publisher access failed [S13]. Offer the desk pass and the primary standards instead.

The Act is already fully in force for every high-risk system. Agree?
Answer

Disagree with "fully" and with "every." The desk pass records general application on 2 Aug 2026 and a later Art. 6(1) high-risk path on 2 Aug 2027, plus earlier phases in 2025 [S6]. You also have not classified this use case under an annex.

Retrieval check

What are the four headings on the only sheet you may hold during the talk?
Answer

Market, Voluntary, Certifiable, Statute. They match TRiSM, NIST AI RMF and AI 600-1, ISO/IEC 42001, and Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

Which sentence must appear in the same breath as the CSA quote?
Answer

The speaker works for a vendor that sells behavioral detection, so the framing has a commercial interest [S12].

Exit criteria

  • One timed delivery, 4:30 to 5:30, notes closed except the four headings.
  • At least four reviewer questions answered without the ledger.
  • The stumble sentence is written down for block 6.
