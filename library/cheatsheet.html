Architecture decision rules for AI-TRISM: altitude, pillars versus layers, standards, and red flows.

Cheatsheet: AI-TRISM

Business Explanation

Business Explanation

When a design review moves fast, people skip from a market slogan to a product name. These rules keep the briefing at the right altitude, attribute analyst structure honestly, and mark untrusted data in red. They are decision rules, not a second copy of the views.

Decision rules only. Sources: Conceptual view through Detailed design, CLAIMS-AND-DEBATES, source-ledger.

Altitude

  • When the sentence names an outcome or an actor, keep it in the conceptual view, because products are not capabilities.
  • When the sentence names a responsibility, an entity, or a payload, put it in the logical view, because hosts and SKUs are still unknown.
  • When the sentence names a zone or a standard, put it in the physical view. In this pack, do not add a SKU, region, or government-cloud claim, because those pages were not read.
  • When the sentence names a package, a path, or a CLI, stop. The detailed design has a build order only. Inventing commands would fake a product.
  • When a detail from a lower view is needed to explain a higher one, move the sentence down. Do not drag the product up.

Pillars versus layers

  • When you are placing enforcement (governance, runtime inspection, information governance, infrastructure), use the four layer names, and attribute them to secondary Market Guide summaries [S9]. [S1] confirms the count and does not confirm the names.
  • When you are talking to an application owner about explainability, ModelOps, application security, and privacy, use the four pillars [S9] [S10], and call them a crosswalk.
  • When someone calls the pillar list "Gartner's four layers," correct the altitude of the words. The intake made that slip. Do not repeat it in the review.
  • When fairness or safety comes up, say they are in the Gartner outcome list [S1] [S8] and not in a named pillar [S2] [S9]. Do not invent a pillar to close Debate 4.

Standards anchors

  • When the room asks "what can we certify," choose ISO/IEC 42001 through an independent certification body [S14] [S15]. ISO does not certify you itself.
  • When the room asks "how do we talk about risk," choose NIST AI RMF Govern, Map, Measure, Manage [S2], plus AI 600-1 for generative risk names [S3]. Both are voluntary. There is no NIST certificate to wave.
  • When the room asks "where do tools sit," choose TRiSM as the capability map. Do not choose it as the audit anchor [S1] [S8].
  • When the system is placed or used in the EU, add Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as law [S6]. Do not recite unread articles. General application cited in the desk pass is 2 August 2026.
  • When you need a threat name, choose the OWASP 2025 list [S7]. When you need a mitigation paragraph, only LLM01 has been read in full [S7b].
  • When you need to show NIST work next to ISO clauses, use the crosswalk [S4]. Do not claim the crosswalk makes NIST certifiable.

EA framework crosswalk (TOGAF · DoDAF · UAF)

  • When the room asks which ADM phase you are in, answer from the view's Framework lens (A/B conceptual, C logical, D/E physical, E/F build order).
  • When the room still briefs in OV/SV language, use the DoDAF column. When the room uses UAF, answer with domain-aspect cells (Op-Sr, Sv-Cn, Sd-Tx), not OV numbers relabeled as UAF [S17].
  • When someone asks whether DoDAF is dead, say R&E points at UAF superseding DoDAF over time and this pack keeps both until a CIO mandate retires DoDAF [S18].
  • When someone confuses the NIST-to-ISO control table with the EA map, separate them: [S4] is standards; ea-framework-crosswalk is architecture frameworks.

Red untrusted flows

  • When the payload is a user or agent prompt, draw it red into the runtime gateway (logical F1, physical client link, CP3) [S7].
  • When the payload is retrieved or embedded content, draw it red into the application (logical F3, physical retrieval link, CP4) [S7]. Indirect prompt injection uses this path.
  • When the payload is a call to a third-party model, treat it as supply chain (LLM03, TB5), not as one of the two red content links, unless you have read a reason to repaint it.
  • When a model output will be acted on by another system, do not call the gray arrow trusted. Improper output handling (LLM05) was not read. Say the downstream handler is unverified.
  • When you are tempted to let the model enforce policy on itself, refuse. The policy engine decides. The model is an untrusted user [S7].

Control shape

  • When you add a gateway, also keep the agent identity broker outside it, because a bypassed or failed gateway must not hand out broad tools [S7] [S12].
  • When an agent is introduced, give it its own identity and just-in-time scope [S12]. A shared service account fails the "who acted" question.
  • When a high-risk action is proposed, require a person [S7]. Least privilege is not the same as unattended action.
  • When compliance is described as a later project, pull evidence capture (F8) forward. The build order starts with the evidence store for that reason.
  • When blocking is proposed on day one, choose monitor-only first. Enforce mode's latency and false positives are UNVERIFIED.
  • When a vendor claims the firewall covers injection, count LLM01: filtering is the smaller half [S7b]. Cite the academic talk for an outer layer [S11] and the keynote for identity limits [S12], including that speaker's vendor interest. Do not cite a detection rate. E3W is unrun.

What not to fill in

  • When a cell is gov-cloud, SKU, region, retention, or quota, write UNVERIFIED.
  • When a Gartner sentence needs the Market Guide PDF, stop. It is still paywalled.
  • When Ray 2026 or the full AI 600-1 risk tables would settle Debate 4, say the test is partial. Do not finish the map from memory.
