Block 2: Conceptual view

Block 2 study guide: capabilities C1 to C8 and actors, with no product names.

Time: 60 minutes

Business Explanation

Business Explanation

Before a tool is bought, someone must say what "managed AI" means in outcomes: we know which systems exist, what data they touch, whether they still behave as approved, and what stops them in production. This block is that list, plus who is trusted to set it and who is not trusted to feed it. Product names wait for a later altitude.

Goal

Redraw capabilities C1 to C8 and the actor trust column from memory, and say which four capabilities are secondary analyst features rather than pack inventions.

Required reading

  • Conceptual view. Read the altitude callout, the actor table, the capability table, and the open questions. Use the diagram as a check, not as a thing to memorize line by line.
  • Debate 1 and Debate 4 in CLAIMS-AND-DEBATES, only the claim rows.
  • Do not open Physical view in this hour. If you need a product to explain a capability, the sentence is in the wrong block.

What to learn

The view's job is what and why. A capability is an outcome. An actor is someone or something with a goal and a trust level. A concept (asset, policy, control, evidence) is named only so later views have a noun.

C1 to C4 are the four features IBM lists from Gartner guidance it dates to February 2025 [S8]. They are not a paste from a Gartner PDF this pack opened:

# Outcome in one line Who feels the pain if it is missing
C1 AI catalog Every model, application, and agent is listed, including shadow AI The executive who cannot answer "what AI do we run?"
C2 AI data mapping Each system has a known read, train, and emit set The privacy officer in an audit
C3 Continuous assurance Behavior is retested, not blessed once at launch The model builder after drift
C4 Runtime inspection and enforcement Policy hits inputs, outputs, and actions while the system runs Security operations during an incident

C5 to C8 are the pack's bridge from the pillar language [S9] and from NIST's trustworthiness characteristics [S2]. They are marked that way in the view. Do not tell a reviewer they came from the same IBM list.

  • C5 explainability and monitoring.
  • C6 model lifecycle governance (ModelOps): version, release, retrain, retire.
  • C7 information and privacy governance on training data, prompts, retrieval, and outputs.
  • C8 compliance mapping and evidence against NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, and applicable law [S2] [S4].

Trust is the other half of the picture. The end user is an untrusted input source. The AI agent is untrusted by default and gets least privilege. The third-party model provider is supply-chain risk [S7]. The auditor does not operate the system. The auditor demands evidence. That last distinction is why C8 is an outcome, not a person.

NIST's seven characteristics are the yardstick in Debate 4 [S2]: valid and reliable, safe, secure and resilient, accountable and transparent, explainable and interpretable, privacy-enhanced, and fair with harmful bias managed. A first mapping in the claims note leaves safe and fair without a named pillar. Leave that gap visible. Do not invent a fifth pillar to close it.

Agency is the degree to which a system acts through tools [S7]. It is why C4 cannot be "a filter on text" only. You will cash that idea in the logical view.

Actions

  1. Close the view. Write C1 to C8 as eight outcomes, one line each. Mark C1 to C4 [S8] and C5 to C8 as derived.
  2. Draw eight boxes and nine actor arrows from memory. Check against the class diagram. Fix only the arrows you missed.
  3. Add one sentence under the drawing: which two NIST characteristics you will not pretend the pillar names cover [S2] [S9].
  4. Strike any product, cloud, or SKU that appeared. Those belong in Physical view or Integration and AI.

Retrieval check

Closed book

Name C1 to C4 and the kind of source they come from. Which actors are untrusted by default? Which two NIST characteristics lack a clearly named pillar?

Answer

C1 catalog, C2 data mapping, C3 continuous assurance, C4 runtime inspection and enforcement. They come from IBM's account of Gartner guidance [S8], not from a Gartner page read in this pack. Untrusted by default: the end user as an input source, the AI agent, and the third-party model or tool provider [S7]. Safe, and fair with harmful bias managed, have no clearly named pillar [S2] [S9].

Exit criteria

  • C1 to C8 redrawn with no product names, matching Conceptual view.
  • C1 to C4 attributed as secondary [S8]. C5 to C8 not quietly moved into that citation.
  • Actor trust column includes user, agent, provider, and auditor.
  • Debate 4 gap (safety and fairness) is still visible.
