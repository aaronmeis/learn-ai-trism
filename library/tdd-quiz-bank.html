Quiz bank: AI-TRISM

Answer closed-book. Grade after. Log the score in Progress Log, multi-pass tracker Quiz column. Each item has a stable id in a comment before it. The study console at learn-ai-trism uses the same ids.

Design review (block 8) should pull at least three of these items. Do not treat a high score as permission to invent SKUs or primary Gartner layer names.

Every Why it matters and Trap line is drawn from pack notes and the source-ledger only. Items marked mc are multiple choice, and their wrong options are real review mistakes taken from the failure modes.

Business Explanation

Business Explanation

The quiz is the proof the study day worked. It asks for capabilities, flows, zones, control points, and the build order, and it asks what the pack must not claim. Folded answers keep the first pass honest, and each answer says why a miss would hurt in a design review.

View 1: Conceptual view (block 2)

  1. Name C1 to C4. What kind of source are they, and what must you not say about that source?
Answer

C1 AI catalog, C2 AI data mapping, C3 continuous assurance, C4 runtime inspection and enforcement. They are IBM's account of Gartner guidance [S8]. Do not say you read those four names on a Gartner page.

Why it matters. C1 to C4 are the capability names this pack keeps reusing, so a reviewer will ask where they came from. A clear source grade up front keeps the rest of the slide credible [S8].

Trap. Presenting C1 to C4 as Gartner's own words. They are the four features IBM lists from Gartner guidance (February 2025 per the conceptual view), on a vendor explainer page [S8].

  1. Which actors are untrusted or external, and what does the auditor want instead of access?
Answer

End user is untrusted input; AI agent is untrusted by default; third-party provider is supply-chain risk [S7]. The auditor wants evidence (C8), not an operator role.

Why it matters. A design that draws the agent and the provider as trusted hides its own attack surface. The conceptual view marks them untrusted before any product exists [S7]. The auditor gets evidence so that audit never depends on access to the operators.

Trap. Giving the auditor an operator role so they can look around. The view gives them evidence (C8) and nothing more.

  1. Which two NIST trustworthiness characteristics have no clearly named pillar in this pack?
Answer

Safe, and fair with harmful bias managed [S2] [S9].

Why it matters. These two gaps are the honest answer when someone says the four pillars cover everything NIST asks for. The map from NIST characteristics to pillars is unfinished, which is why Debate 4 stays open [S2] [S9].

Trap. Saying the pillars cover all seven characteristics. NIST's own text does not require one separate control per characteristic (E4W), but that does not make the pillar names cover safe or fair [S2].

  1. What does the August 2025 Gartner Hype Cycle press release actually say about AI TRiSM's structure?
Answer

Four layers of technical capabilities; outcomes include governance, trustworthiness, fairness, safety, reliability, security, privacy, and data protection. The PR does not name the four layers [S1].

Why it matters. This is the sentence a reviewer with the press release open will test. Four layers is confirmed. Names are not [S1].

Trap. Reading "four layers" as "the four layers we use are Gartner's." The release lists outcomes (governance, trustworthiness, fairness, safety, reliability, security, privacy, data protection) and names no layer [S1].

  1. A slide says "Gartner's four layers are governance, runtime, information, and infrastructure." What is the accurate response?
  • A. Correct. The Hype Cycle press release lists those four layers.
  • B. The press release confirms four layers without naming them. The names come from secondary pages that cite the Market Guide.
  • C. Wrong. Gartner describes four pillars, not layers.
  • D. Correct, as long as the slide also cites NIST AI RMF.
Answer

B. The press release confirms four layers without naming them. The names come from secondary pages that cite the Market Guide.

  • A. The press release does not list layer names. This is the failure mode "Primary Gartner layer names invented".
  • B. Right. [S1] confirms four layers and names none. [S9] attributes the names to the Market Guide, which is paywalled.
  • C. Gartner's release speaks of layers. The pillar names are a separate secondary list.
  • D. NIST does not name Gartner's layers, so citing it cannot make the names primary.

Why it matters. Reviewers test this exact sentence. A correct answer separates what the free source confirms from what a secondary source reports [S1] [S9].

Trap. Agreeing with the slide. It sounds right because the names are widely repeated.

View 2: Logical view (block 3)

  1. Name the request choke point and the second component that must still limit harm if that choke point is bypassed.
Answer

Runtime gateway. The agent identity and access broker on F4, which does not depend on the gateway.

Why it matters. A gateway is one choke point, and a bypass sends everything past it. The broker on F4 does not depend on the gateway, so a bypass still meets a limit. A design is judged by what happens when its choke point fails [S7b].

Trap. Answering with a second filter. Reasoning: it likely shares the gateway's blind spot. Only about two of the seven LLM01 mitigations are filtering; the rest are privilege, architecture, or process [S7b].

  1. Which two payloads are marked untrusted, and which flow number carries retrieved text?
Answer

User or agent prompt, and retrieved text. Retrieved text is F3 [S7].

Why it matters. Reasoning: retrieved text is easy to forget as untrusted because it comes from the company's own store. Drawing F3 red makes indirect injection visible on the diagram [S7].

Trap. Drawing the vector store arrow as a trusted gray line. That is the failure mode "Retrieved content drawn trusted" [S7].

  1. Why does the policy engine sit outside the model, and which flow makes evidence a by-product?
Answer

The model is treated as an untrusted user, so it must not enforce policy on itself [S7]. F8 captures evidence from every component.

Why it matters. The view treats the model as an untrusted user, so policy sits outside it [S7]. Reasoning: otherwise an injected prompt could talk the model out of its own rule. The policy engine decides allow, deny, or escalate, and F8 makes evidence a by-product of every decision.

Trap. Putting the rule in the system prompt and calling it enforcement. That is the failure mode "Model enforces its own policy".

  1. Where should policy enforcement for the assistant live?
  • A. In the system prompt, because the model reads it first.
  • B. In a policy engine outside the model, which treats the model as an untrusted user.
  • C. In the model's fine-tuning, so every response follows the rule.
  • D. In the retrieval index, so unsafe passages are never returned.
Answer

B. In a policy engine outside the model, which treats the model as an untrusted user.

  • A. Reasoning: an injected prompt could argue the model out of its own rule. This is the failure mode "Model enforces its own policy".
  • B. Right. The policy engine decides allow, deny, or escalate outside the model [S7].
  • C. Fine-tuning is part of the model, which the design does not trust to enforce its own policy.
  • D. Retrieval is itself an untrusted flow (F3). It is a place to inspect, not the place that enforces policy.

Why it matters. Where the rule lives decides whether an attacker can talk past it [S7].

Trap. Choosing the system prompt because it is the easiest place to write a rule.

  1. On the logical view, how is the retrieved-text flow (F3) drawn, and why?
  • A. Gray and trusted, because it comes from the company's own handbook.
  • B. Red and untrusted, because retrieved text can carry hidden instructions into the prompt.
  • C. It is not drawn, because retrieval happens inside the model.
  • D. Drawn like the evidence flow (F8), because both are logging paths.
Answer

B. Red and untrusted, because retrieved text can carry hidden instructions into the prompt.

  • A. This is the failure mode "Retrieved content drawn trusted". Internal text can still carry indirect injection.
  • B. Right. Retrieved text is one of the two payloads marked untrusted [S7].
  • C. Retrieval is a separate step that feeds the prompt, and the view draws it.
  • D. F8 captures evidence. F3 carries untrusted content toward the model.

Why it matters. Marking F3 red is what makes indirect injection visible in a review [S7].

Trap. Trusting text because it comes from your own store.

View 3: Physical view (block 4)

  1. Place the evaluation harness, the runtime gateway, the policy engine, and the evidence store into zones.
Answer

Evaluation harness in TB1 build. Gateway in TB2 runtime. Policy engine and evidence store in TB4 governance.

Why it matters. Zones are where a physical review argues. Build, runtime, and governance are separate trust boundaries, so policy and evidence sit in governance instead of beside the thing they watch.

Trap. Putting the evidence store in the runtime zone next to the gateway. In the physical view it belongs to TB4 governance with the policy engine.

  1. Which links are drawn red on the zone diagram, and which external link is a supply-chain risk without being one of those red links?
Answer

Red: client prompts into the gateway, and retrieval from enterprise data into serving. Calls to a third-party model provider are supply-chain risk [S7] and are not those two red links.

Why it matters. The two red links are the boundary crossings that carry untrusted content: client prompts into the gateway, and retrieval into serving [S7]. Reasoning: a reviewer will read red as "inspect here".

Trap. Counting the third-party model call as one of the two red links. It is a supply-chain risk, a different kind of exposure, and the view describes it separately [S7].

  1. Sort NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act, and OWASP by the kind of authority they have. What belongs in a SKU cell?
Answer

NIST AI RMF is voluntary [S2] [S3]. ISO/IEC 42001 is a certification route through independent bodies [S14] [S15]. The EU AI Act is law where it applies [S6]. OWASP is an optional threat vocabulary [S7]. SKU cells say UNVERIFIED.

Why it matters. Mixing authority types is the most common over-claim in this pack: a voluntary framework briefed as a certificate, a market term briefed as law. Sorting them first keeps the rest of the defense honest [S2] [S3] [S6] [S7] [S14] [S15].

Trap. Writing a product name into a SKU cell because it came up in a source. The cell says UNVERIFIED until product docs are read.

  1. What kind of authority is the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications in this pack?
  • A. Law where it applies.
  • B. A certifiable standard.
  • C. An optional threat vocabulary.
  • D. A Gartner-owned framework.
Answer

C. An optional threat vocabulary.

  • A. Law in this pack is Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.
  • B. ISO/IEC 42001 is the certifiable route. OWASP has no certification body in the ledger.
  • C. Right. OWASP is an optional threat vocabulary, with mitigations quoted in full only for LLM01 [S7] [S7b].
  • D. OWASP is a community standard, and Gartner owns none of it.

Why it matters. Briefing OWASP as law or as a certificate is an authority mix-up a reviewer will catch [S7].

Trap. Choosing "certifiable" because a standard sounds like something you pass.

View 4: Integration and AI elements (block 5)

  1. Name four inventory themes. Why is a vendor named in the table not a recommendation?
Answer

Any four of: model supply, inspection products, identity, data catalog/DLP/vector stores, SIEM or ITSM, red-team tooling, auditor evidence. A named vendor only means a source mentioned it: a ledger source or, for Radware and BigID, a search result. No vendor documentation was read.

Why it matters. The inventory maps the categories around an AI system so you know what to ask about. It is a list of questions, not a shopping list.

Trap. Reading a named vendor as a recommendation. A named vendor only means a source mentioned it (a ledger source, or for Radware and BigID a search result). No vendor documentation was read.

  1. Walk CP3, CP4, CP5, and CP6. Which two are red, and what combination stops oversharing?
Answer

CP3 inspects the prompt. CP4 treats retrieval as untrusted. CP5 scopes the tool call. CP6 inspects the output. CP3 and CP4 are red. Oversharing is stopped by C7 labeling (CP1), output inspection (CP6), and least privilege (CP5), not by a system-prompt line alone [S7].

Why it matters. These four control points are the request path in order. CP3 and CP4 are red because untrusted text enters there. Oversharing needs three controls together (labeling, output inspection, least privilege) because no single one covers it [S7].

Trap. Fixing oversharing with a line in the system prompt. Block 5 says it takes C7 labeling (CP1), CP6 output inspection, and CP5 least privilege.

  1. State the agent identity rule. What is known about government-cloud support for the inventory?
Answer

One identity per agent, least privilege, just-in-time scope, human approval for high-risk actions [S12] [S7]. Government-cloud support is UNVERIFIED on every row that applies (the auditor portal row is Not applicable).

Why it matters. Agent identity is the control that still works when filtering fails. UNVERIFIED on the government-cloud rows protects you from briefing a product as cleared when nobody read its docs [S7] [S12].

Trap. Giving every agent one powerful account and expecting the gateway to refuse bad calls. That is the failure mode "Shared agent service account". Also label the [S12] speaker's vendor interest.

  1. Of the seven OWASP LLM01:2025 mitigations, about how many are filtering, and what does that do to a gateway-only design?
Answer

About two are filtering or output validation. About five are privilege, architecture, or process [S7b]. A gateway stays on the design; it does not replace least privilege, human approval, segregation of external content, or adversarial testing.

Why it matters. A design that leans on the gateway alone overlooks that about five of the seven LLM01 mitigations are privilege, architecture, or process [S7b]. That is the case for keeping the identity broker.

Trap. Quoting a detection rate for the gateway. E3W has not been run and no ledger source measures one.

  1. Three agents share one service account for tool calls. What does the pack say to do?
  • A. Keep the shared account and let the gateway refuse bad tool calls.
  • B. Give each agent its own identity with least privilege, just-in-time scope, and human approval for high-risk actions.
  • C. Raise the model's refusal threshold so risky calls are never attempted.
  • D. Keep the shared account and review the logs every month.
Answer

B. Give each agent its own identity with least privilege, just-in-time scope, and human approval for high-risk actions.

  • A. This is the failure mode "Shared agent service account". The gateway is not the only limit that should apply.
  • B. Right. One identity per agent, least privilege, just-in-time scope, human approval for high-risk actions [S12] [S7].
  • C. That is a model setting, and the design does not rely on the model to limit its own actions.
  • D. Logs help afterward. They do not limit what a shared account can do.

Why it matters. Identity is the control that still limits harm when filtering fails [S12] [S7].

Trap. Picking the gateway option. It feels like a control and leaves every agent with the same power.

  1. What does the integration inventory say about government-cloud support for the listed products?
  • A. Supported on every row.
  • B. Supported on rows that carry a FedRAMP mention in the source.
  • C. UNVERIFIED on every row that applies.
  • D. Not supported on any row.
Answer

C. UNVERIFIED on every row that applies.

  • A. Nothing in the ledger shows government-cloud support. Briefing it would be the failure mode "SKU / gov-cloud invented".
  • B. A mention in a source is not proof of support for a product row.
  • C. Right. Government-cloud support is UNVERIFIED on every row that applies. The regulator and auditor portals row is Not applicable.
  • D. "Not supported" is also a claim. The honest status is that nobody read the docs.

Why it matters. UNVERIFIED keeps a briefing from implying a clearance nobody checked.

Trap. Reading a named vendor as proof the product is cleared.

View 5: Detailed design (block 6)

  1. Why is the evidence store step 1 and the asset registry step 2?
Answer

In the pack's proposed build order, evidence is step 1 so that later discovery can be shown, because a scan that is not recorded cannot be shown later. The registry is step 2 so policy or blocking has an asset to attach to. Catalog-first matches the secondary feature list [S8].

Why it matters. The build order is the pack's own proposal, and it is an argument: a discovery scan that is not recorded cannot be shown later, and policy needs a registered asset to attach to. Catalog-first matches the secondary feature list [S8].

Trap. Starting with the gateway because it is the visible control. The view puts evidence first and the registry second, before policy has anything to attach to.

  1. What is monitor-only for, and what remains UNVERIFIED about enforce mode?
Answer

Monitor-only learns real traffic before blocking. Latency and false positives of enforce mode are UNVERIFIED. Fail-closed versus fail-open is per use case.

Why it matters. Monitor-only gives you real traffic before anything is blocked. Latency and false-positive effects of enforce mode are UNVERIFIED, so "no impact" is a claim without a source.

Trap. Blocking production traffic on day one. See the failure mode "Enforce mode on day one".

  1. Which designed test is still unrun, and what kind of GO does it need? Name one artifact this pack refused to draw.
Answer

E3W, the guardrail comparison, needs an install GO. The pack refused to author 05-detailed-design.drawio without a chosen realization.

Why it matters. E3W is the designed test that would put a number on guardrails against a plain least-privilege setup, and it needs an install GO. Until it runs, the pack refuses detection rates and the final drawing.

Trap. Saying E3W was run, or that the missing drawing is an oversight. The pack declined to author 05-detailed-design.drawio until a realization is chosen.

  1. A team wants to block production traffic on day one. What does the pack recommend?
  • A. Enable enforce mode now, because blocking is the point of a gateway.
  • B. Run monitor-only first to learn real traffic. Latency and false positives in enforce mode are UNVERIFIED.
  • C. Enable enforce mode only for the EU region.
  • D. Skip monitoring, because the red team already covers it.
Answer

B. Run monitor-only first to learn real traffic. Latency and false positives in enforce mode are UNVERIFIED.

  • A. This is the failure mode "Enforce mode on day one". Nobody has baseline traffic yet.
  • B. Right. Monitor-only learns real traffic before blocking. Fail-closed versus fail-open is per use case.
  • C. The pack gives no region-based rollout rule. Nothing in the ledger supports it.
  • D. A red team is a test before release. It does not replace learning production traffic.

Why it matters. Blocking without a baseline turns unknown false positives into outages.

Trap. Assuming enforce mode has no cost. Its latency and false-positive rates are UNVERIFIED.

Cross-cutting (blocks 1, 7, and 8)

  1. Give one sentence each for conceptual, logical, and physical altitude.
Answer

Conceptual: outcomes and actors, no products. Logical: parts, records, and flows, no hosts. Physical: zones and standards, SKUs unknown on purpose.

Why it matters. Altitude keeps a product name out of a conceptual slide and a vague claim out of a physical one. One sentence per altitude is the oral test in design review.

Trap. Putting SKUs on the physical view. They stay unknown on purpose, and filling them from memory is a named failure mode.

  1. When do you use the layer names, when do you use the pillar names, and how do you attribute both?
Answer

Use secondary layer names when you place enforcement [S9], and say [S1] did not name them. Use pillar names as the application crosswalk [S9] [S10]. Never present either list as a primary Gartner quotation.

Why it matters. Layers place enforcement. Pillars crosswalk application concerns. Using the wrong word for the job confuses the room, and presenting either list as primary Gartner text is a claim the pack forbids [S1] [S9] [S10].

Trap. Quoting layer or pillar names as Gartner's own words. Both lists are secondary.

  1. A sponsor says the AI firewall covers prompt injection. What do you concede, what do you add from LLM01, and what do you refuse to claim?
Answer

Concede that an outer inspection layer is a serious proposal if the model cannot be fixed [S11], and that LLM01 includes filtering [S7b]. Add least privilege, human approval, segregation of external content, and per-agent identity [S7b] [S12]. Refuse a detection rate, and note the [S12] speaker's vendor interest. E3W is unrun.

Why it matters. This is the sponsor conversation the pack prepares you for. Conceding what is true keeps you credible, and the non-filter mitigations do the persuading [S7b] [S11] [S12].

Trap. Refusing to concede that an outer inspection layer is useful. Bo Li's order is risk assessment and red team first, then an outer firewall if the model cannot be fixed [S11].

  1. Name the three architecture languages in the EA crosswalk. Give one UAF 1.3 cell for the conceptual view. How does that crosswalk differ from the NIST-to-ISO table?
Answer

TOGAF ADM, DoDAF viewpoints, and UAF 1.3. Conceptual UAF cells include Sm-Ov, St-Tx, St-Mv, or Op-Sr [S17]. The EA crosswalk places architecture elements; the NIST-to-ISO table [S4] maps control ids. Keep DoDAF alongside UAF [S18].

Why it matters. TOGAF ADM, DoDAF, and UAF 1.3 place architecture elements. The NIST-to-ISO table maps control ids. Merging them produces one certificate story that neither table supports [S4] [S17] [S18].

Trap. Saying DoDAF is retired and only UAF remains. Keep DoDAF alongside UAF until a CIO mandate retires it.

  1. Who certifies an organization to ISO/IEC 42001, and what certification route did the desk pass find for NIST?
Answer

Independent certification bodies certify the AIMS; ISO does not. ANAB accredits the bodies [S14] [S15]. No NIST certification scheme was found for AI RMF 1.0 or AI 600-1; both are voluntary [S2] [S3].

Why it matters. A sponsor who says "ISO certified us" will repeat it to an auditor, and the first follow-up is which accredited certification body issued the certificate [S14] [S15].

Trap. Saying ISO or NIST certifies. ISO does not certify organizations, and no NIST certification scheme was found for AI RMF 1.0 or AI 600-1 [S2] [S3].

  1. State this console's day-end self-check in one sentence.
Answer

Do I still treat secondary pillar or layer names as primary Gartner quotes, or believe TRiSM itself has a certification path? If yes, the day did not change what I would say. This is a console self-check built from the cannot-claim list in the claims note. The intake's own falsifier is different: sources that are only vendor marketing.

Why it matters. A self-check built from the claims note's cannot-claim list. If you still brief secondary names as Gartner quotes, or believe TRiSM can be certified, the day did not change what you would say.

Trap. Passing the quiz and still using the slide with named layers in a review.
