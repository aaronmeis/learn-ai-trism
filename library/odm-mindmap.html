Business Explanation

Business Explanation

An architect who walks into a design review with only a vendor slide will defend the wrong object. AI TRiSM is a market category. The review survives when the design is anchored to a certifiable management system and a voluntary risk framework, with the statute named where it applies. This map is the study path for that defense.

AI-TRISM

  • Day outcome: Design and defend a vendor-neutral AI TRiSM reference architecture. Explain the four layers as a placement map, map controls to risks, and position tool categories in a room.
  • Depth: Working fluency for a design review. Videos and primary docs, desk exercises only. No installs.
  • Prior knowledge: Near zero on AI TRiSM. Start from definitions and from what each source is allowed to prove.
  • Boundaries: Must include explainability and monitoring, ModelOps and lifecycle, AI application security, and privacy and compliance. Cross-check Gartner's framing against NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, and the EU AI Act. Out of scope: training models, single-vendor product tutorials, unread Ray 2026 contents, and any claim that a free Gartner page names the four layers.
  • Core concepts
  • AI TRiSM is Gartner's name for technical capabilities that support governance, trustworthiness, fairness, safety, reliability, security, privacy, and data protection. The 2025 Hype Cycle press release says "four layers" and does not name them [S1].
  • Four pillars, secondary only: explainability and model monitoring, ModelOps, AI application security, privacy. AWS Builder and Zscaler attribute these to Gartner. No Gartner-owned page in the ledger lists the pillar names [S9] [S10]. Some writers add regulatory compliance as a fifth pillar [S9].
  • Four layers, secondary names plus a primary count: AI governance, AI runtime inspection and enforcement, information governance, infrastructure and stack. Zscaler attributes the names to the Market Guide. The Market Guide PDF is still paywalled [S1] [S9].
  • IBM's page is a third cut: glossary language plus governance, AI data protection and classification, and runtime inspection and enforcement [S8]. A public Gartner article frames TRiSM as technical controls beyond policy. The article is gated mid-page [S16].
  • NIST AI RMF 1.0: functions Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. Seven characteristics: valid and reliable, safe, secure and resilient, accountable and transparent, explainable and interpretable, privacy-enhanced, fair with harmful bias managed. Voluntary [S2].
  • NIST AI 600-1: July 2024 GenAI profile, voluntary companion to AI RMF. Intro read. Risk tables not fully mined [S3].
  • ISO/IEC 42001:2023: first AI management-system standard. An AIMS can be certified by independent certification bodies. ISO does not certify. ANAB accredits bodies and cites ISO/IEC 42006:2025 [S5] [S14] [S15].
  • EU AI Act, Regulation 2024/1689: binding where it applies. Recorded classes: prohibited practices Art. 5, high-risk Chapter III, GPAI Chapter V, transparency Art. 50. General application 2 Aug 2026. Art. 6(1) high-risk path 2 Aug 2027 [S6].
  • Market vs standard vs law: TRiSM places tools. NIST supplies risk vocabulary. ISO/IEC 42001 is the conformance anchor. The Act is the statute.
  • Mental models
  • Pillars vs layers. Pillars are an application crosswalk. Layers are where a control sits. Preference for layers is the architect's convenience, recorded as Debate 1, not a finding. E1A and E1B are partial.
  • Design against standards, use TRiSM as the capability map. Debate 2. No published design in the desk pass treats TRiSM as the sole auditable anchor. ISO is certifiable. NIST has no certification scheme in the ledger.
  • Runtime inspection vs architecture. Debate 3. An outer firewall is what you add when the model cannot be fixed, after red teaming [S11]. Filters do not cover an unbounded input space, and agents need identity and least privilege [S12] [S7b]. About two of seven LLM01 mitigations are filtering. About five are privilege, architecture, or process.
  • Trustworthiness gap. Debate 4 is still partial. Pillar names do not say fairness or safety. NIST lists both. Do not close that gap from memory.
  • Active practice targets
  • Map one AI use case onto the four secondary layers and say which names are secondary.
  • Map OWASP LLM01 through LLM10 onto those layers. Mark LLM01 mitigations as read and the other nine entries as names only [S7] [S7b].
  • Map Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage onto control owners. Use the TDD logical components as a proposal, not as a NIST diagram.
  • Teach-back angle
  • Five minutes for a design-review room: four authorities, then where controls sit, then what you refuse to claim.
  • Killer answers ready for "Gartner named these layers," "certify to TRiSM," "the firewall is enough," and "cite Ray 2026."
  • Edge cases / traps
  • Guardrails-only designs. Shadow AI with no catalog. Agents on shared high-privilege accounts.
  • Paywalled analyst terms repeated as if the room had read the Market Guide.
  • Vendor interest in the CSA keynote. The speaker's employer sells behavioral detection [S12].
  • Collapsing market category, voluntary framework, certifiable standard, and statute into one slide.
  • Application task
  • One-page vendor-neutral reference architecture for a named use case.
  • Sections: system in one sentence, four layers with controls, standards anchors, tool categories, evidence the room can ask for, claims you will not make.
  • Tool categories come from the TDD logical and physical views: registry, data map, policy engine, runtime gateway, agent identity broker, evaluation harness, monitoring, evidence store. No SKUs.
