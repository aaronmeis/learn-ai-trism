Block 5: Edge cases

Block 5: traps that make an AI TRiSM design look finished when it is not.

Time: 45 minutes Goal: Catch five traps and say the room sentence that stops each one. Pack: AI-TRISM hub

Business Explanation

Business Explanation

The expensive failure is a design that looks governed because a product name is on the diagram. These traps are the specific ways that happens with AI TRiSM: a filter standing in for architecture, invisible systems, agents with too much access, analyst terms the room has not read, and a sales talk filed as independent evidence.

Trap 1. Guardrails-only designs

Pattern. The diagram shows an AI firewall between users and the model. There is no identity broker, no human approval, no separation of retrieved content, and no red team.

Why it fails. Bo Li wants that outer layer when the model cannot be fixed, and he puts red teaming first [S11]. The CSA keynote states the limit in plain language: you cannot do filters and rules on an infinite number of inputs and outputs [S12]. OWASP's seven LLM01 mitigations put filtering in the minority [S7b].

Room sentence. Inspection stays on the diagram, after a red team, and beside least privilege, human approval, and segregated retrieval content.

Do not add. A detection-rate percentage. E3W has not been run.

Trap 2. Shadow AI

Pattern. The approved assistant is on the catalog. A team also pastes customer text into a personal chatbot, or an agent runs from a notebook the registry has never seen.

Why it fails. The TDD conceptual view takes the catalog from IBM's account of Gartner guidance so the risk owner can see every model, application, and agent, including shadow AI [S8]. Policy on the approved path does not touch the invisible one. Information governance cannot label data it never sees.

Room sentence. Discovery plus registration is a control, with an owner and a risk tier. A system that is not in the catalog is outside the AIMS evidence, whatever the gateway does for the systems that are listed.

Trap 3. Agent over-permissioning

Pattern. One service account can read the handbook, open tickets, read mail, and update payroll. Every agent uses it. The gateway is expected to refuse bad tool calls.

Why it fails. The CSA talk's starting question is who the agent is and what it can access [S12]. OWASP tells you to keep the model's privileges to the task and to put extended functions in code, with a person approving high-risk actions [S7b]. The TDD logical view splits the access broker from the gateway so a missed filter does not equal full privilege. That split is a proposal. The privilege rule is not.

Room sentence. Each agent gets its own identity, scoped to draft a ticket or to answer from the handbook, and payroll is not on that identity at all.

Trap 4. Paywalled analyst terms

Pattern. The slide says "per Gartner's four layers" and lists governance, runtime inspection, information governance, and infrastructure. Nobody in the room has the Market Guide. The free press release is not quoted.

Why it fails. [S1] supports the count and the outcomes. It does not support the names. [S9] supports the names as a citation to the Market Guide. E1B is partial. Treating the names as primary text is how a review adopts a paywalled claim at blog strength.

Room sentence. Gartner counts four layers on the 2025 press release and does not name them there. The names on this slide are the secondary Market Guide wording, and the PDF is still paywalled.

Same trap, pillar edition. "Gartner's four pillars" from an AWS or Zscaler page is secondary [S10] [S9]. E1A is partial.

Trap 5. Vendor interest in talks

Pattern. The room treats the CSA keynote as a neutral lab result, then buys the speaker's product category as if the transcript were a controlled test.

Why it fails. The transcript, via auto-captions, does support a limit of filters and a focus on agent identity [S12]. The speaker's employer sells behavioral detection. The claims note records that interest. Bo Li's talk is a university talk and does not carry the same interest flag [S11]. Neither talk reports a measured detection rate.

Room sentence. Use the keynote for the limit it states. Label the commercial interest. Do not treat it as a bake-off. E3W is still the lab you have not run.

Retrieval check

What do you keep, and what do you add, when a design is guardrails-only?
Answer

Keep runtime inspection. Add red teaming first, least privilege, human approval for high-risk actions, and segregation of external content [S11] [S7b]. Quote the filter limit from [S12] with the vendor-interest label.

What makes shadow AI invisible to the rest of the design?
Answer

It is missing from the catalog, so ownership, data mapping, and evidence never start [S8]. Gateway policy on registered systems does not see it.

Which three claims are still off limits after this block?
Answer

Named layers quoted as Gartner's own free-page text. A TRiSM certification. Any finding from Ray 2026 [S1] [S13] CLAIMS-AND-DEBATES.
